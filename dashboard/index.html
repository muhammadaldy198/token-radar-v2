The v2 fix: conditional odds — P(ever launches | tokenless at this age)

F(t) is the share of protocols already tokened by each age, measured on 525 peers. The conditional odds are (F∞ − F(t)) / (1 − F(t)) with F∞ = 0.95. The 4–5y and 5y+ buckets are pooled (n = 49) because the raw 5y+ bucket held only 6 protocols and broke the monotone fall. Protocols with no listing date use the cohort base rate (59%).

Cumulative incidence says an old protocol is "likely" — but a protocol that survived four years tokenless has already outlasted most of its cohort. v1 overrated the old and underrated the young by ~3× each way. v2 fixes that, and keeps the logistic fit as a quality ratio — a protocol that looks more launchable than its age peers keeps its edge.

    What v2 changes

    v1 used the fitted cumulative probability as "odds ever". That is the wrong number for a tokenless protocol: it counts launches that already happened in the cohort. The cure model fixes it — every protocol either launches eventually (fraction F∞) or never (1 − F∞), so P(ever | tokenless at age t) = (F∞ − F(t)) / (1 − F(t)). The final odds multiply the conditional age odds by a quality ratio: the protocol's own fitted probability over its age bucket's rate. Age sets the baseline; the protocol's features move it up or down.

    F∞ is not identified — honestly

    The current-status likelihood (each peer contributes F(t) if tokened, 1 − F(t) if not) rises monotonically to the F∞ = 1.00 boundary; the data cannot pin the cure fraction down. v2 uses 0.95, the midpoint of the honest range [0.90, 1.00]. The profile:

    Honest limits

    • The value fit is weak. R² ≈ 0.11 — TVL, chains and growth explain little of market cap. The conformal interval is ±3.2× and bootstrap rank ranges are wide (some top-10 rows span 100+ ranks). Treat ranks as a shortlist, not a prophecy.
    • F∞ is a choice, not a measurement. The likelihood can't identify it; 0.95 is a midpoint. The sensitivity table above shows the board barely moves.
    • Age is a proxy. The cure model assumes protocols launch on a clock. Real launches follow funding rounds, market cycles and team decisions the snapshot cannot see.
    • Survivorship. Dead protocols leave the snapshot; the peers are the survivors. F(t) is measured on survivors only.
    • Farm economics are a plan, not a promise. The 7% airdrop share, the 0.02% deposit and the $960 cost are assumptions; the sensitivity grid shows what happens when they're wrong.
